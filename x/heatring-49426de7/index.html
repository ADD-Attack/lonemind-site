02 · The ring

Far heat is the hard case.

Imagine a metal ring split into 64 cells. Random spots are heated: the heat sources. Heat flows to neighbouring cells, but it also leaks into the air at a rate called the damping, κ ("kappa"). After a while the temperatures stop changing. That resting pattern is the steady temperature, and for this toy problem we can compute it exactly, so we can grade every model.

Weak damping (κ = 0.1) means little leakage, so heat from one source is felt far around the ring. That is the hard case: a cell must "hear" sources many cells away.

The plain nets are ordinary residual conv nets. They guess the answer in one shot, with no passes to spend.

03 · Passes and q

q is the certificate.

StasDEQ's one block is applied again and again. Each application is one pass, and it repeats until the answer stops changing. A block only looks at neighbouring cells, so one pass moves information a short hop. For a far-away source to reach a cell, you need many passes.

A number called q (between 0 and 1) is a proven bound on how fast the passes settle. Low q settles fast, but information only travels a short way. High q, close to 1, settles slowly, but information gets all the way round. The passes needed grow roughly like 1/(1−q). Because q is proven to stay below 1, the process is guaranteed to settle, and to the same answer from any starting point.

04 · The q-cap control

More passes, more reach.

Here q is capped during training, so the model may only settle in a limited number of passes. A low cap settles in few passes but heat cannot travel far enough; a high cap needs many passes, and the far cells finally hear each other.

06 · Honest limits

Where it falls short.

An uncertified loop overthinks.

The same kind of repeated block, without the certificate, drifts if you let it run longer than it was trained for. In the report, at κ = 1.0 on 256 cells the error was 0.085 at 256 passes and 4,967 at 1,024 passes (mean of 3 seeds; seeds range 0.0941 to 14,899.4656). The certified core cannot do this: it refuses q ≥ 1 by construction.

Real uncertified loop (runs/H-k1.0-loop-s1…s3.pt), 256 cells, κ = 1.0, three seeds, error vs passes, log scale.

A plain net 8× bigger wins stage 3.

In stage 3 the material's conductivity a(x) changes from input to input (κ = 0.3). StasDEQ still beats the same-size plain net and is the only arm that does not get worse on the bigger ring, but the 8×-bigger plain net is better at both sizes. Also not claimed

  • 1-D toy rings only; no comparison with an FNO or a classical solver (which solves this problem exactly, so the use case is learned operators with unknown physics, not replacing FFT).
  • Prefix parity (a different test) failed for every arm, StasDEQ included.
  • With a cap of 0.8 or below, StasDEQ loses to the 8× plain net and is near the same-size plain net on the long-range ring: the win needs high q.
  • On the short-range ring (κ = 1.0) the 8× plain net is as good or better (0.0016 / 0.0014 vs 0.0021 / 0.0025).
  • Per-sample numbers on this page come from seed 1 only; the report's tables are 3-seed means.
