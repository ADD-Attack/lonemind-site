Six ways a model can think.

01 · A regular LLM, from the outside

It guesses one word at a time.

Text goes in as tokens (pieces of words). The model scores every possible next token, picks one, adds it to the end, and repeats.

Temperature is a dial. Low means it nearly always takes the favourite. High means it takes more chances.

Drag temperature all the way down: the top bar swallows the rest.

02 · Inside a transformer

Every word looks back. Then it's done.

Inside, every token looks at every earlier token and decides how much each one matters. That is "attention".

It happens in a stack of layers, and the trip up the stack is one fixed pass: the same amount of work for an easy question or a hard one.

Step through: the pass climbs one layer, then a new word takes its turn.

03 · The building blocks inside Liquid's hybrids

Not one block repeated. A mix.

Liquid AI's models mix several kinds of block, each moving information differently. Section 4 shows how LFM2 stacks them. Section 6 is a proposed LoneMind design that mixes block types the same way and adds exact, rule-based parts.

In LFM2 [1,2]

A · Gated short convolution

Each token looks at only a few neighbours. Gates made from the input scale the signal before and after. Cheap. LFM2 uses 10 of these.

In LFM2 [1,2]

B · Grouped-query attention

A token can look at every earlier token. Several query heads share one set of keys and values, which saves memory. LFM2 uses 6 of these.

In Liquid's STAR search space [3] · not in released LFM2

C · Linear recurrence

Reads tokens one at a time into a fixed-size running state: new state = keep × old state + input. Memory never grows.

The umbrella family [1,3]

D · LIV operators

Linear input-varying: a linear operation whose weights are made from the input itself. Here three mixing weights are rebuilt for every token (blue), where an ordinary layer keeps them fixed (grey).

Liquid's research roots [4,5] · not listed as LFM2 blocks

E · Liquid neurons: LTC and CfC

A liquid time-constant (LTC) neuron reacts at a speed set by its input, and is simulated with many small steps (grey dots). The closed-form (CfC) version writes the answer down directly at any time (green points), so it is much faster.

Sources: [1] Liquid AI, “Introducing LFM2”, 10 July 2025: 16 blocks, 10 double-gated short-range convolution + 6 grouped-query attention; describes LIV operators. [2] LFM2-1.2B model card, Hugging Face: 16 layers (10 conv + 6 attn). [3] Thomas et al., “STAR: Synthesis of Tailored Architectures”, arXiv:2411.17800 (2024). [4] Hasani et al., “Liquid Time-constant Networks”, AAAI 2021. [5] Hasani et al., “Closed-form continuous-time neural networks”, Nature Machine Intelligence 4, 992–1003 (2022). 04 · Liquid AI

A neuron whose speed changes with the input.

Liquid AI grew out of liquid time-constant neurons: tiny continuous-time units whose reaction speed changes with the input. Strong input makes them snap quickly; weak input lets them drift.

Their language models (LFM2) are a hybrid: mostly short-convolution blocks for local patterns, with a few attention blocks for long-range lookup. LFM2-1.2B has 10 convolution + 6 attention [1,2].

Push input strength up: the green neuron's time-constant shrinks and it snaps faster.

05 · stasDEQ · LoneMind's certified equilibrium model

It loops until it settles, and proves it.

Bytes pass through a small encoder, which makes a nudge c. Then one shared core runs over and over: z ← relu((1−a)z + a(Wz + c)), with W = (1−m)I − AᵀA + (B−Bᵀ). That recipe for W is built so the loop tends to settle.

Every pass is checked. The stretch q of one step must be under 1; then the distance left to the settled answer is at most q/(1−q) × the last step's movement. If q ≥ 1, the model refuses to certify instead of guessing.

About 2.4M weights in total, about 262K in the core, a 512-number state.

Push twist to the right until the gauge crosses 1. Watch it refuse.

06 · stasHybrid · proposed design

Send each job to the right kind of neuron. Proposed

A proposed LoneMind design, not built or tested. Instead of one kind of neuron everywhere, mix three:

Deterministic parts do jobs that have a known exact rule. Short-attention neurons look only at a few neighbours: cheap, good for local patterns. Wide-view neurons can look across the whole input: expensive, used only where needed.

A router sends each piece of work to the right kind. It borrows Liquid's idea of mixing block types (section 3) and adds exact parts.

The routing rule here is a toy. Proposed design.
